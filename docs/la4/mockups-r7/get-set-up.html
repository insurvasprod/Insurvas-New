Get set up · round 1

Owner 2026-11-12: Get set up is "one of the last things of UI UX changes, and one of the most important". It walks through the whole SaaS, covers only the modules the account pays for, and gives each kind of user their own setup, so anyone can set everything up alone. Defaults keep it short. Three directions follow. Each is drawn for an owner on a full agency plan (Ellery & Fields: Outbound, Inbound, Applications, Book, Retention, Compliance, Accounting, Agency), a solo agent (Ray Delgado, Basic individual plan: Outbound, Applications, Book) and non-owner roles (producer Dana Whitfield, plus setter, assistant, bookkeeper and partner user in C), with every panel, pop-up, sheet, toast, state and phone view each direction opens. Research, analysis and sources: docs/redesign/get-set-up-r1.md.

  • Rules applied on every board: a trail and title line on every page; tabs and buttons sit on the title line over one hairline; layout is fluid and full width; one line per table row, one text colour, colour only in one StatusChip, and the next step written as a verb plus ›; no notice or Callout cards (anything that needs attention goes to the bell); no glow, gradients or magic icons; text is 12 / 14 / 16 only.
  • Status chips: Done On defaults (an Insurvas default applies, which counts as done: the work really is in place) · To do · Waiting on carrier or Waiting on Ellery (lead time, not the reader's work) · Skipped · With Paula (handed to someone).

R3 · Round 3: one next step, then Insurvas shows you every click

The owner on round 2 (2026-11-12): "too tabley… so many clicks… no redirection on what to do next". When he clicks Add your carriers and it opens Settings, it should walk him through it: click this first, then this, then do this. Every step works that way.

  • Get set up has no tables and no tabs. There is one Next step and one big Start button. Below it is the path, one line per step. Clicking any line starts that step, so nothing opens in between.
  • Start opens the real page with the guide running. A setup bar sits under the title line. The page dims except for the one control to touch, and a coach next to it says what to do. The guide moves on when you do the action, so there is no Next to press, except on a step that only asks you to check something.
  • Finishing a step takes you straight to the next one. "Done · Next: Add your states ›" opens that page with its own guide already running, so you only see Get set up again if you choose to.
  • This replaces R2's phase tables (R2·0–R2·2) and settles the walkthrough choice (R2·3 / R2·4): the guide is pinned to the control. Reports › Team setup (R2·6–R2·7) stays.

Getting appointed with carriers

6 min read · 3 min video·Owners and producers·Updated Nov 2026
▶
VideoGetting appointed, start to finish
3:12

An appointment is a carrier saying you may sell its products. The carrier files it with each state you sell in. Your licence lets you sell insurance; an appointment lets you sell that carrier's policies.

What the carrier asks for

  1. Your resident licence number, plus any non-resident licences
  2. An E&O certificate, often $1M per claim
  3. An AML training certificate, sometimes the carrier's own product training
  4. A background check and your bank details for commissions

Direct or through an IMO

Most new agents contract through an IMO or FMO. It sends one packet to many carriers, often gets you a higher contract level, and helps with underwriting questions. Going direct means a separate contract with each carrier.

How long it takes

Usually one to three weeks per carrier. Some states need you appointed before you take an application; others accept it when the first application arrives. Your IMO knows which.
