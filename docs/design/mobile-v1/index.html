What the phone is for

The web app has about 290 pages because it runs an agency end to end. The phone should not try to. It should do the work people do away from a desk, and tell them the moment something needs them. Everything else stays on the computer and the phone knows how to send you there.

1 · Work the phone

Take a transfer, serve the next lead, keep every callback in the customer’s time. The phone is a phone: dialing hands off to the native dialer after the server’s compliance checks pass, and transfers ring like calls.

Who: producers, setters, assistants.

2 · Close and follow the sale

Quote at the kitchen table, fill the application with the client, sign on the glass, then watch the case through underwriting. Save the policy when the carrier file says a payment was missed.

Who: producers, retention.

3 · Know where the money is

Owners: the month, the team, and the approvals waiting on them. Producers: what they earned and what came back. Partners: what they sent and what it became. Read and approve on the phone; reconcile on the computer.

Who: owners, managers, bookkeepers, partners.

Rules that carry over from the web

  • Nothing printed on the page as a notice. On the phone the bell becomes the Inbox tab and push. It fits the owner’s rule better than the web does.
  • Calls never use a raw tel: link. They go through the dialer: server checks, disclosure record, then the native call.
  • Customer’s time and yours, together, on every callback and appointment.
  • Roles are fixed. Each role gets its own five tabs, and the money boundary holds: setters and assistants never see lead cost.
  • One gold button a screen, gold for people and new counts. Green acts, olive frames. Status is a dot plus words.
  • No client avatars, no emoji, no glow, no gradients, no endless motion. Sentence case everywhere.

What the phone adds that the web can’t do

  • Push alerts for transfers, callbacks, case changes, at-risk policies and chat, with actions on the notification.
  • A transfer that rings: iOS Live Activity and Dynamic Island, Android full-screen call notification.
  • Face ID / fingerprint sign-in on top of the 30-day trusted device.
  • Camera: a receipt straight into Expenses, a document straight into a pending case.
  • Sign on the glass for applications taken in person.
  • Home-screen widgets and long-press shortcuts: today’s callbacks, Serve next lead, Add a lead.
  • Works with a weak signal: notes, dispositions and application steps save on the phone and send when it reconnects.

How each page was placed: phone or computer

Every page was scored on six questions. Two or more “phone” answers and it is built for the phone; mostly “computer” and it stays on the web, reachable from Menu.

Engineering choices behind it

  • The server stays the judge. Compliance, roles, plans and money rules run on the same API as the web; the app never decides who may call or see cost.
  • Offline only where it is safe: notes, outcomes and application answers queue on the phone. Claiming, dialing and approving always need the server.
  • Sensitive data stays off the phone: bank and SSN masked after entry, Face ID to show, nothing cached in plain text, app-switcher cover, lock after a pause, remote sign-out.
  • Every push opens one exact screen through deep links that match the web routes, so an email link and a push land in the same place.

What I left off the phone, on purpose

  • Bulk work: imports, mass reassignments, bulk stage moves, merging lead histories.
  • Setup that changes things for everyone: pipelines, products, carriers, field maps, routing, roles, disclosures.
  • Side-by-side money work: statement matching, credit attribution, splits, tax.
  • Plan and billing: Apple and Google require their own payments for anything bought inside the app.
  • Texting clients from a personal phone: it bypasses consent and recording, so the app doesn’t offer it.

Decisions, settled 24 Nov 2026

  1. One app for agents and partners. One store listing, one codebase; the sign-in decides which side opens.Settled: one app. Partners see only their portal; nothing of the agency side ships to them.
  2. Text sizes on the phone. Larger than the web’s 12 / 14 / 16, as Apple and Google recommend.Settled: 13 / 15 / 16 body, 20 section titles, 28–32 screen titles and big figures. Follows the phone’s text-size setting.
  3. Rows: two lines on the phone. On a 390px screen, one line hides the state or the time.Settled: a name line plus one muted fact line, colour only in one chip. The web keeps its one-line rule.
  4. Buttons. Android’s own buttons are fully round, which the web forbids.Settled: 10–12px corners on both platforms. No pills.
  5. Type face.Settled: Inter and Inter Tight everywhere, Geist Mono for figures, so the product looks like one product.
  6. Applications on a phone.Settled: the full application, with signature, for final expense, whole life and term. Products on the generic template show a laptop mark and send the link to the computer (screen E1b).
  7. Tabs for an owner who also sells.Settled: owner tabs by default, with Menu › Settings › “Use the producer tabs” (screen J3). Until release 3 brings Team and Money, owners get the producer tabs.
  8. What ships when.Settled: three releases, below. Every screen on this board carries its release mark, and the toolbar can show one release at a time.

What building it needs

  • Expo (React Native) with expo-router, so the phone’s routes mirror /app/*; EAS builds for both stores.
  • Sign-in for an app: the web uses cookies. The phone needs token sign-in on the same session rules (remember, trusted device, session_version), kept in the Keychain / Keystore.
  • Push: device tokens per user and a sender for the bell’s existing categories (APNs + FCM). This is the “SMS / push” gap already on the premium list.
  • Shared rules: the pure modules in lib/ (call phases, outcomes, ladder rules, money formatting) move into a package both apps import, so the phone can’t drift from the web.
  • Same API, same gates: every screen calls the existing /api/app routes, so menu, page and API keep agreeing.

Motion on the phone

  • The calm-premium tokens carry over (120–360ms, rises and fades, nothing endless).
  • Native pushes and sheets use each platform’s own spring, so iOS feels like iOS and Android like Android.
  • One haptic tap on Claim, Call and Save outcome; a warning buzz when dialing is blocked.
  • Reduced motion on the phone turns every transition into a fade.
