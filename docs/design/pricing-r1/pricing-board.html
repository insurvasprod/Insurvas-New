2 · The Three Comparison Options

A · What Changes (recommended)Only the rows where the plans differ, about 15 instead of 48, under a plan header that stays on screen. Price and seats are the first two rows. One button shows the 22 features every plan shares, greyed. Same pattern as Linear, Attio and Notion: the cards say "everything in X, plus", and the table answers "what do I lose by staying down".
B · By ModuleNine rows, one per module, each cell a count ("All 8", "5 of 8", "—"). Click a row and its features open under it (Retention is open in the drawing). The shortest first view. The cost: you have to open a row to see what a count means.
C · By Job, in WordsEight rows named after the work ("Keep policies on the books"), each cell a short sentence instead of ticks. "See Every Feature" opens a side panel with one plan's full list (drawn open below the table). Reads like Intercom's key-features list. Hardest to keep in step with the catalog: the sentences are written copy, so they must be updated whenever a plan's features change.

3 · The Current Comparison Table, Critiqued

It is 48 rows longThe catalog has 39 features in 9 modules, so the table is 39 feature rows plus 9 module headers. That is about 1,900px of ticks, and nothing on screen says which plan a column is by row 30. The plan header does not stick.
Most rows say nothingOn the Agent view, Basic and Advanced share 22 of their 30 features. Three rows in four are a tick in every column, and the 8 that matter are scattered across 5 modules.
The same list appears three timesThe cards list each plan's features by module with "+N more". "Compare table" lists them again, and "Explain each feature" a third time. Two switches lead to the same content.
It cannot cross audiencesThe table shows one audience at a time. An Advanced agent cannot see what Agency adds, which is the most important upgrade.
Ticks only, no valuesSeats, price and "Talk to us" are not rows, so the facts a buyer compares first are missing. Labels such as "Discrepancy report" and "Draft-date optimiser" have no explanation.

4 · What Premium Pricing Pages Do (Research, 2026-11-19)

Short cards, an increment listNotion, Attio and Close show 3–4 cards with one "best for" line and one badge. Each card starts "Everything in X, plus" and lists only what it adds.
The full matrix sits below, groupedClose (about 50 rows in 6 sections) and Attio (about 70 rows, "Expand all") put the matrix below the cards in named groups. Vercel shows 150+ rows in full, which nobody reads end to end.
Values in the cell, not extra rowsNotion writes "5 MB" and "5 GB" in the cell, and Vercel prices add-ons inside the cell. Limits are not split into extra rows.
Seats are priced next to the planAttio prints "Add users for $35/mo each" on the card. Vercel prices seats separately from the plan. Intercom gives free "Lite" seats with each plan. None hides the seat cost in a footnote.
Custom means no numberLinear, Notion and Vercel show "Custom" with Contact Sales and "Everything in Business, plus". HubSpot shows "Starts at".
The yearly switch changes prices in placeNotion says "Save up to 20% with yearly", Attio "Save 20%", and Intercom shows a crossed-out monthly price. The saving is printed on the switch itself.
