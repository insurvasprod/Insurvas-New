Settings › Dialing cadence

Dialing cadence decides when the outbound dialer calls a lead again after a call that did not connect: how long it waits, which part of the day it tries, after which outcomes, and when it gives up. Today it is a table of numbered rules ("attempt N is the wait before dial N"), a bare campaign picker, and up to four notices stacked above it. You cannot see the shape of the schedule, and you cannot see where Sundays, holidays or state limits will move a call.

1 · The numbering needs a lessonRule N is "the wait before dial N", attempt 1 is a locked row with no wait, and a rule past max attempts is stored but never read. People think in calls on days, not attempt numbers.
2 · A table hides the shapeThe stat strip says "4 · 2 · 2 spread", but nothing shows that half the calls land in the first three days or that call 6 falls on a Sunday.
3 · Calling windows are invisibleNo Sunday, federal holidays, Florida's three-calls-in-24-hours and per-campaign hours live on another page. The cadence promises calls the dialer will quietly move.
4 · Four notices push the editor downSave errors, max-attempts errors, read-only and "pending update" are Callout cards. Owner rule: those belong in the status line or on the row they are about.
5 · Scope is a bare select"Every campaign (tenant default)" versus a campaign name, with no sign which campaigns run their own cadence, and switching drops your edits.
6 · Outcomes are snake_case scopes"no_answer", "callback" per row, with duplicate-rule errors. Owners only want to say "after a voicemail, wait longer".

A · Visual timeline (recommended)The cadence becomes a 14-day timeline with calls as dots in morning, afternoon and evening rows. Drag to change a wait, click to edit, presets plus a max-calls stepper, and a sentence that reads the result back.
  • New vs today: no attempt numbering to learn. Call 1 is just the first dot.
  • New: Sundays, holidays and campaign hours drawn as striped areas; moved calls turn orange on the timeline and in the status line, not as notice cards.
  • New: Gentle / Balanced / Persistent presets, outcome tabs (Voicemail differs…), and a "What happens" list beside the timeline.
  • New: scope picker that shows which campaigns run their own cadence; history with changes in words; a reset dialog that says who is affected.
B · Rules in plain sentencesEach step is a sentence ("If still no answer, call again after 2 days in the afternoon") with inline dropdowns, one tab per outcome, and a calls-per-day bar strip.
  • New vs today: reads like a policy; nothing to decode.
  • Closest to today's data model, so the cheapest build.
  • Weaker: you still read the shape rather than see it; moved calls appear only as chips on a step.
C · Simulator firstPick a campaign and an outcome pattern, see a two-week calendar of exactly what the dialer does, and edit from the calendar; rules sit in a side panel.
  • New vs today: answers "what will happen to my leads" directly, including when the cadence stops on an answer.
  • Best for checking calling-window conflicts per campaign.
  • Weaker: editing one example lead changes the rule for every lead, which can surprise people; needs the warning in the popover.
