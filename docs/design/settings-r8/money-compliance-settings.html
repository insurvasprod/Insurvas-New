Settings › Money & compliance · Accounting, Retention, Compliance

These three pages hold the rules behind the money and the regulator: when money counts, how a policy at risk climbs the save ladder, how long a complaint has before a regulator expects an answer. Today they look like three different products. Accounting and Retention open a pop-up per row; Compliance is a long form with one save bar for everything. This board puts all three on one pattern: a one-line setting row that opens a right sheet to change that one setting, with what it affects and its history inside.

1 · Two save models side by sideAccounting and Retention save one setting per dialog; Compliance saves the whole page from one bar at the foot. Three pages in one rail group, two ways to keep a change.
2 · Dialogs hide the consequenceThe Accounting dialog shows the value and a Save button. Who uses it, who changed it last and what it was before live elsewhere (More › History), so a change is a guess.
3 · Sub-pages swap the whole screenRetention weights, Compliance deadlines, holds and purge runs each replace the page with a back arrow. You lose the list you came from.
4 · Locked rows don't say why"Only the owner changes these" and "Insurvas never sends money" read as broken buttons. Nobody says it is a safety rule, or who to ask.
5 · Compliance is one long formResponse deadlines, record ages, holds and gate checks run as stacked FormSections; the save bar covers twelve unrelated settings, so one typo blocks the rest.
6 · No sign of "yours" vs "Insurvas"Retention has Reset to Insurvas values, but nothing on the row shows which values you changed. You have to open each one to know.

What changes vs today

  1. One save model for all three. Every setting saves on its own from its sheet. Compliance loses its page save bar; Accounting and Retention lose the centre pop-ups.
  2. Rows say more. Now in plain words, what uses it, who changed it last (gold avatar), and one chip: Yours, Insurvas or Locked.
  3. History moves into the sheet. The last few changes with Put back; "See every change" opens Settings history filtered to that section. The More › History menu becomes a History button.
  4. Locked rows explain themselves. The sheet says why, what it means day to day and who to ask. Non-owners see the value and history, not a dead button.
  5. Sub-pages become sheets. Retention /weights → wide sheet with live band preview and versions. Compliance deadlines/new and /[regulator], retention/[recordType], holds/new and /[id], runs/[id] → right sheets over their tab.
  6. Compliance regrouped into tables. Response deadlines, record ages, holds, runs and deletion rules are each a card with one-line rows, not stacked form sections.
  7. Tab counts. Complaints and Holds & deletion keep their counts in the header tab.
  8. No database change. Each sheet calls the same one-setting save the dialogs use today; Compliance needs a per-field save in place of its whole-form PATCH.
