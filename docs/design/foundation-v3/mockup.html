Nine sans-serif typefaces from finance and YC-backed product teams

Decision: Inter (Inter Tight for headings and big numbers) is the foundation typeface. The others stay here for reference. Every option is a sans-serif family, the style banking and fintech apps use for screens and numbers. The fonts many fintechs are known for (Söhne, Aeonik, Lausanne) are paid licences, so each card pairs the look with a free Google Font we can self-host through next/font today. Pick one and the whole page re-sets in it, including every card, table and popup below.

Attributions come from public design write-ups and font-in-use listings, not from the companies' own brand guides. Treat them as reported, not confirmed. Every face here supports tabular figures, which keeps money columns aligned.

Design critique

What the review changed

Run against the UI Taste product-interface checklist: hierarchy, density, honest data, one component vocabulary, overlays chosen by task.

KPI cards had no hierarchy

BeforeFour equal boxes: big number, small label, a delta with no comparison.NowPremium leads with period, comparison, target and daily shape. Policies show composition. Everything else lives in one divided strip.

Money was hard to scan

BeforeProportional digits, cents at full weight, mixed alignment.NowTabular figures, muted cents and currency, right-aligned money columns, one format: $1,284.00.

Status relied on color

BeforeColored text pills, with each screen choosing its own colors for the same state.NowDot plus label, a fixed meaning per hue (green done, blue scheduled, amber needs action, red stopped).

Every popup was a modal

BeforeDetail views and confirmations both interrupted the page.NowSheet for reading records, Dialog for short edits, Alert Dialog only when something is lost, Toast with Undo for confirmations.

Cards were used as wallpaper

BeforeNested panels and borders around every group.NowA card marks one object (a policy, a person, an event). Inside it, spacing and a single divider do the grouping.

Monospace was decoration

BeforeMono used for whole tables to look technical.NowMono only for identifiers people copy or read aloud: policy numbers, Medicare IDs, plan codes.
